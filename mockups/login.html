Bulwark Mobile · sign-in redesign

Three fields nobody knows the answer to

The current sign-in screen opens with a Server URL field. Most people don't know their JMAP endpoint, and the two paths that don't require knowing it — the webmail handoff and the QR code — sit below a divider, greyed out until the URL is filled in anyway. This proposes an entry screen that leads with the easy paths and asks for a server only when we genuinely can't work it out.

Today

Everything at once, in the least helpful order. The webmail handoff is the safer route — it never puts a password into the app — but it's disabled until you've supplied the server, so it can't rescue anyone who's stuck.

Proposed flow

One question per screen, cheapest question first. The screens below are the happy path, left to right.

When it doesn't go smoothly

Self-hosted servers, typo'd passwords, and second accounts are the normal case for this audience, not the exception. These four screens carry most of the redesign's weight.

Notes for the build

  • Discovery is three cheap probes, in order

    From ada@example.com: try https://example.com/.well-known/jmap, then https://mail.example.com/.well-known/jmap, then the same host the last account used. First 200 wins. Budget the whole thing at two seconds and fall through to Path B on timeout — a slow lookup must never be worse than the manual form.

  • Nothing new on the wire

    Every screen drives an existing store action — loginViaWebmail, loginViaPairing, login. The redesign is routing and copy; the auth surface, the state machine, and parseQrLoginPayload stay as they are.

  • Back must always work

    A multi-step flow earns a hardware-back handler on Android. Every step goes back one, and step 1 leaves the flow — never a trapped screen with a filled-in field you can't clear.

  • Let the OS help

    autoComplete="email" and textContentType="username" on the address field, "password" on the fallback field, so the platform password manager offers to fill and save. The current screen sets neither.
